Welcome to Damascus

Damascus, the City of Jasmine, is the oldest continuously inhabited city in the world. It is known for its Roman gates and colonnades, its Ottoman-era courtyard houses, and streets and churches named in the Bible. We'll stay in and explore the Old City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site inside the Roman walls, with its souqs, the chapels and churches of the Christian quarter, the Umayyad Mosque, and the famous Khan As'ad Pasha, an 18th-century merchants' inn built in striped black-and-white stone beneath eight domes.

In the souqs we'll shop for mother-of-pearl boxes and backgammon sets, Damascene brocade, hand-blown glass, Aleppo soap, and rosewater.

Where We'll Stay

The hotels in the Old City are restored Damascene houses, known for their marble courtyards with fountains, citrus trees, and jasmine. Every morning we'll have coffee and a traditional Syrian breakfast there. Potential hotels include Beit Zafran, Beit Zaman, and Beit Al Wali.

Who We'll Meet

Contemporary Artists

Studio visits with Syrian painters and sculptors, and galleries set inside old houses, such as Kozah on Straight Street and The Khan, a restored caravanserai that reopened this year as a crafts store and gallery.

Startup Founders

A visit to the headquarters of one of Syria's leading technology startups and time with its founders on what it takes to build a company in Syria today, and where the opportunities are.

Policymakers

Conversations with the people shaping Syria's reconstruction, from government ministries to the investment authority, on how the country is reopening its economy and where it goes from here.

Syria Is Open

The Syrian civil war is over, the former regime fell, and foreign governments have lifted sanctions. Damascus airport is served by Qatar Airways, flydubai, Turkish Airlines, and others, and US citizens get a 30-day visa on arrival at the airport or at the Lebanese border. Syria received 3.5 million visitors in the first half of 2026. It's an exciting time to visit the country.
